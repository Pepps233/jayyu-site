When we observe something and want to understand it, where should we begin?
In [*On Contradiction*](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-193708.htm), written in 1937, Mao Zedong offers a way to approach this question: begin with the contradictions within the thing itself, and its connections with the things around it.[^en-text]
Reading this essay is also a way to learn how materialist dialectics can be applied to the analysis of specific things.

At the beginning, Mao quotes Lenin: “In its proper sense, dialectics is the study of contradiction within the very essence of things.”[^en-text]
Here, contradiction means more than an argument between people; it concerns the opposing yet interconnected aspects within something, and the relations between them.
In the essay's account, contradictions exist in every process of development and persist throughout it.
This makes me wonder: when facing a situation, are we so accustomed to looking for external causes that we overlook what is happening within it?

## Seeing internal problems through external competition

The essay begins by discussing two world outlooks: metaphysics and dialectics.
Drawing on Lenin's distinction between two views of development, Mao explains that the former sees development as quantitative increase, decrease, and repetition, while the latter understands it through the unity of opposites.
“Metaphysics” here refers to the isolated, static, and one-sided way of examining things that the essay criticizes.[^en-worldviews]
Mao writes:

> Metaphysicians believe that the different things in the world and their characteristics have been as they are since they first came into existence.
> Subsequent changes are merely quantitative expansion or contraction.
> They believe that a thing can only repeatedly produce the same kind of thing, and cannot change into a different kind of thing.[^en-worldviews]

Mao also discusses the relationship between this outlook and capitalist society, which I will not explore point by point here.
What I want to understand more clearly is the relationship he describes between internal and external causes.
In his analysis, internal contradictions provide the basis for development, external influences provide conditions for change, and external causes operate through internal causes.[^en-worldviews]

Imagine two competing companies, each hoping to create a better product than the other.
Competition might create pressure and encourage them to accelerate research and development.
Yet under the same competitive pressure, one company might improve its products while another stagnates or leaves the market.
The existence of competition alone does not explain why they reach different outcomes.

If we take one company as our object of analysis, we also need to examine its internal relations.
For example, what gap exists between its product ambitions and its current technical capabilities, what tension exists between the need to expand research and its limited funding, and how are these problems being addressed?
Seen this way, whether external competition leads to actual progress is connected with the company's internal conditions.
For me, the point of this example is a reminder: when studying external pressure, also ask through which specific internal relations it takes effect.

## Does universal contradiction mean everything has a fixed opposite?

Mao's account of the universality of contradiction has two dimensions: contradictions exist in the development of all things, and they persist throughout each process.
The essay quotes Engels: “Motion itself is a contradiction.”[^en-universality]

Reading this, I naturally think of action and reaction, life and death, success and failure, up and down.
Without failure, how would we understand success?
Without up, how would we define down?
These examples help me see how some concepts and relations become understandable through their connection with an opposite.

But taking this thought further requires care.
The universality of contradiction cannot simply become an exercise in finding a fixed antonym for everything.
Contrasting success with failure cannot replace investigating the specific causes of a particular success or failure.
What we need to ask is: within the process we are discussing, which aspects constrain one another, and through what relations are they connected?

Looking back at my own life, I can recognize many desires and demands pulling in different directions.
But if I want things to change in a favorable direction, I still need to understand how these tensions arose and which conditions can be changed.
Simply saying that something will turn into its opposite cannot make difficulty turn into favorable circumstances on its own.

## The principal contradiction changes with conditions

Although contradiction is universal, each contradiction also has its own particularity.
Different things, different stages of development, and different aspects of the same contradiction require concrete analysis.
When an old process ends and a new one begins, the structure of contradictions and the methods for resolving them also change.[^en-particularity]

Many contradictions can coexist within a complex process.
In Mao's account, at a given stage of that process, one plays the leading role, determining or influencing the development of the others: the principal contradiction.
“Principal” therefore concerns its position and role within the process; it cannot be identified simply by looking for the most intense conflict.
The principal contradiction also differs from the principal aspect of a contradiction: the former distinguishes the leading contradiction among several, while the latter identifies the dominant side within a single contradiction.[^en-principal]

The changing relationship between the Kuomintang (KMT) and the Communist Party of China (CPC) offers a historical example.
After their cooperation broke down in 1927, military and political confrontation between them was prominent during the Agrarian Revolutionary War period.
In Mao's analysis, this confrontation was connected with class relations within China and cannot be understood merely as a disagreement between two political parties.[^en-particularity]

The subsequent shift did not begin abruptly with the outbreak of nationwide resistance to Japan in 1937.
After the Mukden Incident in 1931, Japan's aggression against China continued to expand; as the crisis in North China deepened in 1935, the national contradiction between China and Japan became more prominent, prompting changes in the political situation and strategies for resisting Japan.
In December that year, the Wayaobu Conference established the policy of building a national united front against Japanese aggression.[^en-wayaobao]
The peaceful settlement of the Xi'an Incident in December 1936 became an important turning point toward renewed KMT-CPC cooperation.
After nationwide resistance began in July 1937, cooperation advanced further; on September 22, the KMT's Central News Agency published the CPC's declaration on cooperation, and Chiang Kai-shek issued a statement the following day, marking the formal establishment of the national united front based on KMT-CPC cooperation.[^en-chronology]

In the explanation offered by *On Contradiction*, as resistance to Japan became the foremost task, the contradiction between China and Japanese imperialism assumed the principal position, while domestic class contradictions temporarily took a secondary and subordinate position.
The contradiction under discussion was between China and the aggressor, rather than between imperialist powers.
KMT-CPC cooperation did not eliminate their existing contradictions; the essay also recognizes the complexity of continued struggle within their alliance.[^en-principal]

This shift helps me see why we cannot permanently fix a relationship in the form it takes at one particular stage.
We need to keep asking: how has the situation changed, which contradiction now plays the leading role, and where do the others stand?

## Looking more comprehensively, and more concretely

To consider a problem thoroughly, we need to study the different aspects of its contradictions as objectively as possible.
The one-sidedness Mao criticizes includes “understanding only China and not Japan, only the Communist Party and not the Kuomintang,” as well as “understanding only favorable circumstances and not difficult ones.”[^en-method]

This passage makes me think about how often our analysis begins with the side we know best and most want to believe.
But if we know only our own reasons and not the other side's circumstances, or see a success without examining the conditions that made it possible, our judgment may remain superficial.

Studying a problem takes time, analyzing its contradictions takes time, and understanding its internal relations and patterns takes still more time.
If we want to do something well, this work needs to be taken seriously.

Toward the end of Section III, Mao also discusses the relationship between generality and individuality.
I still need more time to work through this part.
But a starting point is that generality concerns properties shared by different concrete things, while individuality concerns their particular characteristics; the general exists within the particular, and studying concrete things is how we come to understand what they share.[^en-common]
Their connection therefore needs to be understood through the relationship between the general and the concrete; saying that everything has an opposite is insufficient to explain it.

## Transformation depends on conditions

Finally, I want to consider the transformation of opposing sides under particular conditions.
In *On Contradiction*, identity concerns both the conditional interdependence of opposing sides within a unity and their conditional transformation into the position occupied by the opposite side.[^en-identity]

Mao uses the agrarian revolution as an example: landlords who owned land lost it, while peasants who had little or none acquired land and became small private owners.
The discussion concerns landlords and peasants, and their changing positions in relations of landownership.[^en-identity]
Acquiring land does not make peasants proletarians; nor does losing land, by itself, mean that landlords have become the peasants who previously lacked it.
Understanding this transformation still requires examining specific social relations and conditions.

War and peace provide another example in the essay.
Mao understands waging revolutionary war as preparing conditions for eliminating war, expressing both his political objective and his understanding of the conditions needed to achieve it.[^en-identity]
I cannot infer from this that every war will bring permanent peace.

For me, the important reminder lies in the word “conditions.”
Relations between opposites are living and changeable; recognizing this requires further investigation into why change is possible and how it can occur.
Difficulties do not disappear merely because we understand an idea, and a turning point does not arrive simply because we hope for it.

## Returning to daily life

Bringing these reflections back to daily life, I want to begin with something small: when a problem arises, leave room to examine my first reaction.

For example, if my studying is going nowhere, I can ask whether the most pressing problem is weak foundational knowledge, an unsuitable method, or insufficient rest.
If I have not understood the basic concepts, simply doing more exercises may leave the central issue untouched.
When I disagree with someone, I can first understand each person's goals and concerns before deciding how to respond.
The essay also distinguishes contradiction from antagonism, reminding us that the forms of struggle require concrete analysis.[^en-antagonism]
This encourages me to try resolving everyday disagreements through discussion, revised arrangements, or a different division of responsibilities.

For me, the value of reading *On Contradiction* is that I am beginning to ask more seriously: what are the internal relations of the problem, what currently plays the leading role, and which conditions might I change?
If you also have something that remains unresolved, consider this: is the place where you keep directing your effort actually the heart of the problem?
